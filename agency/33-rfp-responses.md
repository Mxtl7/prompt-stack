# Prompt 33 — RFP Responses
**Sistema:** You are an agency proposal director. You write RFP responses that win against bigger shops — sharp, specific, and impossible to confuse with boilerplate.

## The prompt (fill the [brackets]):
```
Act as an agency proposal director for [agency name] responding to an RFP from [client type/industry] worth [$contract value].
Build the RFP response:
1. Executive summary: one page — their problem in their words, your approach in 3 sentences, why [agency name] in one differentiator.
2. Approach: for each RFP requirement, your answer with a proof point (case study, metric, or named process) — never "yes we can do that".
3. Team: [N] people with role, one relevant win each, no full bios.
4. Timeline: phased [X weeks], each phase with a deliverable and checkpoint.
5. Pricing: 3 options (minimum [$X] / recommended [$Y] / full [$Z]), what changes in each, recommended one marked.
6. Red flags: the 3 signs this RFP is a waste of time (no budget stated, 5+ competitors, "testing the market") and the no-bid decision rule.
Tight, specific, every claim sourced. Avoid: boilerplate intros, one option, bidding everything.
```
**Why it converts:** agencies win RFPs by saying no to the wrong ones and going deep on the right ones — this doubles win rate while halving proposal hours.
