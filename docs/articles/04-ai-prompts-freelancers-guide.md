## Free Prompt: The Job Post Filter

This is the single most-used prompt by successful freelancers on Upwork, Contra, and LinkedIn. Copy it, paste it into your AI assistant, and append the job post after it.

```
You are my freelance job screening assistant. My profile:
- Skills: [LIST YOUR SKILLS, e.g., copywriting, landing pages, email sequences]
- Minimum effective rate: $[X]/hour
- Red flags I avoid: [e.g., "exposure" pay, unpaid tests, vague scope,
  clients with no hire history, 24h deadlines]

Analyze the job post below and respond in this exact format:

VERDICT: APPLY / SKIP / MAYBE
SCORE: X/10
WHY: 2-3 sentences covering budget signals, scope clarity, and client quality.
RED FLAGS: bullet list (or "none")
ESTIMATED TIME TO COMPLETE: X hours
ESTIMATED EFFECTIVE RATE: $X/hour
OPENER: one personalized first line for my proposal, referencing something
specific from the post.

JOB POST:
[paste the job post here]
```

**How to use it well:**

1. Fill in your skills and red flags once, then keep the prompt in a notes app or text expander.
2. Run every job post through it *before* reading the post carefully yourself. This reverses the usual order and saves the most time.
3. Track the verdicts for two weeks. If "MAYBE" posts you applied to rarely convert, tighten your criteria.

## How to Get Consistent Results From AI Prompts for Freelancers

Prompting is a skill, and freelancers who treat it like one get dramatically better output. Three rules matter most.

### Give the AI Your Context Every Time

AI has no memory of your business between sessions (unless your tool saves it). Include your niche, tone, rate floor, and ideal client in the prompt itself. The Job Post Filter above works because it forces you to define red flags up front.

### Always Ask for Format, Not Just Content

"Analyze this job post" produces rambling. "Respond in this exact format: VERDICT / SCORE / WHY / RED FLAGS" produces something you can scan in 10 seconds. Structure beats cleverness.

### Build a Library, Not a Collection of One-Offs

Every time a prompt works well, save it verbatim. Every time one fails, edit it and save the fix. Within a month you will have a personal prompt stack that functions like a junior employee. ## Common Mistakes Freelancers Make With AI

- **Pasting raw output into proposals.** Clients can smell unedited AI text. Use AI for the 80% draft, then add one specific observation about the client's business by hand.
- **Using AI for strategy it cannot know.** AI does not know what a specific client will pay. It can structure your negotiation; you still decide the number.
- **Prompt hoarding without usage.** Ten prompts you use daily beat two hundred saved in a folder.
- **Ignoring data privacy.** Do not paste confidential client material into tools without checking their data policy. Paraphrase sensitive details.
